# Research sources

These sources informed the skill's distinction between a storyboard, a shot
list, scene breakdown, and continuity handoff.

- Adobe, "How to get started on storyboarding": <https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/video/discover/storyboarding.html>
- Adobe, "How to make a shot list": <https://www.adobe.com/in/creativecloud/video/discover/shot-list.html>
- Adobe, "What Is a Storyboard?": <https://www.adobe.com/in/products/firefly/discover/what-is-a-storyboard.html>
- StudioBinder, "Shot List vs Storyboard": <https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/shot-list-vs-storyboard/>
- StudioBinder, "How to Storyboard a Short Film": <https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/how-to-storyboard-a-short-film/>
- StudioBinder, "How to Make a Shot List": <https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/how-to-make-a-shot-list/>
- CANVAS, a recent continuity-aware visual storyboarding research direction:
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13452>

Use these as design references, not as rigid screenplay rules. Adapt shot density,
timing, and camera grammar to the chosen video model and the user's genre.
